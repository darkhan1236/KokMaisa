# Kazakhstan Pastures And Agriculture Reference 2026

Source freshness: gathered on 2026-05-18 from official Kazakhstan Government, legal, FAO, World Bank, and Kazakhstan statistics sources. Use this as KokMaisa RAG background for Kazakhstan pasture and livestock questions. Do not present it as live data after 2026-05-18.

## Kazakhstan Pasture Management Status

The official information source of the Prime Minister of Kazakhstan reported on 2026-05-06 that Kazakhstan is returning unused and illegally allocated agricultural land to state ownership and putting returned land back into agricultural circulation.

Key figures from the Government update:

- Since the beginning of 2026, 354 unscheduled inspections covered about 1 million hectares.
- 261 orders were issued to eliminate land-law violations, covering nearly 500,000 hectares.
- More than 563,000 hectares of agricultural land had been returned to state ownership.
- A phased redistribution plan runs until mid-2026: by 2026-04-20, 10.8 million hectares out of a planned 13.5 million hectares had been put into circulation.
- The national pasture deficit had been reduced to 3.2 million hectares over the previous two years.
- Regions still lagging on pasture provision included Aktobe, Atyrau, Mangystau, and Ulytau.
- Government instructions included completing pasture management plans, financing pasture infrastructure, strengthening public explanation work, and increasing control over unused land.

KokMaisa interpretation: when advising a user in Kazakhstan, treat pasture shortage, rational land use, fodder-base planning, and degradation prevention as current policy priorities. Do not claim that all local pasture deficits are solved; the official status says shortages remain, especially around some settlements.

Source: Official Information Source of the Prime Minister of Kazakhstan, "Over 563,000 Hectares of Agricultural Land Returned to State Ownership", 2026-05-06. URL: https://primeminister.kz/en/news/over-563000-hectares-of-agricultural-land-returned-to-state-ownership-31357

## Pasture Law And Plans

Kazakhstan's law "On pastures" defines the legal basis for pasture use, pasture infrastructure, pasture users, and local pasture management. The legal record notes amendments including the Law of 2024-02-27 No. 65-VIII and the Law of 2025-04-09 No. 179-VIII, with parts entering into force on 2025-07-02.

Practical KokMaisa rule: for legal or administrative questions, the AI should not give definitive legal advice. It can say that pasture use is regulated through official pasture legislation and local pasture management plans, and recommend checking the current akimat plan or official Adilet text.

Source: Adilet Legal Information System, Law of the Republic of Kazakhstan "On pastures". URL: https://adilet.zan.kz/eng/docs/Z1700000047

The Ministry of Agriculture order dated 2024-07-29 No. 263 approved a typical plan for pasture management and use. This matters for KokMaisa because recommendations should align with planned rotational use, sustainable fodder base formation, and prevention of degradation.

Source: Adilet Legal Information System, Minister of Agriculture order No. 263, 2024-07-29. URL: https://www.adilet.zan.kz/rus/docs/V2400034831

## FAO Pasture Management Notes

FAO describes livestock as one of the leading agricultural sectors in Central Asia, including Kazakhstan. FAO notes that about 72 percent of the region's area is rangeland, but management quality is a major issue. FAO training for Kazakhstan covered sustainable pasture management, desert pasture animal husbandry, forage production in dry conditions, and field-school approaches for livestock farmers.

KokMaisa interpretation:

- Sustainable pasture management means using pasture over the long term without harming the environment.
- Dry regions need special attention to water points, grazing pressure, forage reserves, and seasonal movement.
- Field-school or advisory formats are useful because pasture decisions depend on local plant cover, soil, water, livestock type, and season.

Source: FAO Family Farming Knowledge Platform, "Kazakhstan works toward securing precious pastures for future generations", 2020. URL: https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1317311/

## Climate Adaptation For Kazakhstan Pastures

The World Bank's May 2024 report "Republic of Kazakhstan: Climate Adaptation Options and Opportunities in the Agriculture Sector" identifies agriculture, water, forestry, and disaster-risk reduction as adaptation priorities. It reports that Kazakhstan warmed faster than the global average in the referenced climate period and discusses risks for crops, pastures, and livestock.

Pasture and livestock adaptation themes relevant to KokMaisa:

- Agronomic maintenance and improvement of pastures.
- Mapping grazing areas with GIS.
- Combining forage quality, water availability, shade, temperature, and animal-condition signals for rotation decisions.
- Decision support for when animals should move to another grazing area.

KokMaisa interpretation: AI recommendations should connect biomass, plant coverage, and pasture trend data with practical rotation and recovery decisions. If KokMaisa has no local measurements for a specific pasture, the AI must say that a reliable pasture-specific recommendation needs user measurements, area, livestock load, and season.

Source: World Bank, "Republic of Kazakhstan: Climate Adaptation Options and Opportunities in the Agriculture Sector", May 2024. URL: https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099060424004022607/pdf/P50211216a88d304191601df346d0b1713.pdf

## Livestock And Agricultural Enterprises

The Bureau of National Statistics reported livestock structure data for 2025. As of 2025-09-01, cattle were distributed across households, peasant or farm enterprises and individual entrepreneurs, and agricultural enterprises. The reported shares were 45.7 percent in households, 44.1 percent in peasant or farm enterprises and individual entrepreneurs, and 10.2 percent in agricultural enterprises. For sheep, the shares were 39.9 percent, 53.4 percent, and 6.7 percent respectively.

KokMaisa interpretation:

- Pasture pressure is not only an agricultural-enterprise issue; households and peasant farms represent a large part of livestock ownership.
- Recommendations for KokMaisa should work for smallholders, peasant farms, and larger enterprises.
- When the user asks about "агрохозяйства", distinguish between private user farm data in KokMaisa and national statistics.

Source: Bureau of National Statistics of Kazakhstan, "The main indicators of the development of livestock in the Republic of Kazakhstan (January-August 2025)". URL: https://stat.gov.kz/en/industries/%20business-statistics/stat-forrest-village-hunt-fish/publications/439096/
